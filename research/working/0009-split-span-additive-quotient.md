## Result and exact objects

Fix an arbitrary field `k` and a finite label set `I`. Retain the actual
split-presentable span-class submonoid `S_sp(Z_I/k)`, its free rational
algebra `C_sp(Z_I/k)`, and the previously constructed surjective algebra map

```text
χ_sp : C_sp(Z_I/k) → M_I(Q),
χ_sp([S,l,r]) = ∑ s∈S E_(r(s),l(s)).
```

Define the finite point-decomposition differences and their generated
two-sided ideal independently of this map's kernel:

```text
d_(S,l,r) := [S,l,r] − ∑ s∈S [{*},l(s),r(s)],
J_sp := two-sided ideal generated by all d_(S,l,r).
```

The generators range over every finite split presentation of an actual
geometric class, including empty carriers. The new theorem proves

```text
J_sp = ker χ_sp,
C_sp^add(Z_I/k) := C_sp(Z_I/k) / J_sp,
χbar_sp : C_sp^add(Z_I/k) ≃ₐ[Q] M_I(Q).
```

The free source, explicit relation ideal, quotient, and matrix algebra
remain separate definitions. In particular, the relation ideal is not
defined to be the kernel, nor is the additive quotient defined as matrices.
The generators here are finite indexed point decompositions. Empty and
binary-coproduct defects are proved to belong to `J_sp`.

The map `χ_sp` is proved noninjective (`realization_not_injective`):
the empty-span basis vector is nonzero in the free algebra and maps to
zero. Consequently `J_sp ≠ ⊥` (`additiveRelations_ne_bot`). This supplies
an explicit witness to the information discarded by the coefficient map.

## Geometric provenance of the relations

`SplitPointGeometry.pointDecompositionIso` constructs an actual scheme
isomorphism

```text
Spec(k^S) ≅ ∐ s∈S Spec(k^PUnit).
```

The point inclusions respect both legs. Consequently
`span_pointDecomposition_iso` identifies the original span with the actual
indexed scheme coproduct of its singleton spans. The theorem
`pointDecomposition_hasFiniteEtaleLegs` also verifies that coproduct's legs.
This uses the existing scheme coproduct, not a second abstract coproduct
object or a label-count identity alone.

The singleton carrier `Spec(k^PUnit)` is canonically isomorphic to `Spec k`.
The general decomposition includes the empty scheme. There is no
compactification, boundary, purity, or Chow input in this argument.

## Kernel proof and quotient construction

The linear normal-form map sends a matrix to the same rational combination
of actual point-pair span classes:

```text
N(M) := ∑ i,j M(j,i) [{*},i,j].
```

It is only a linear map into the free source. It is not asserted to be an
algebra-homomorphism inverse to `χ_sp` before taking the quotient.

Every generating difference has zero incidence matrix, so
`J_sp ≤ ker χ_sp`. Conversely, the proof `sub_pointNormalForm_mem` shows
for every free source element `x` that

```text
x − N(χ_sp(x)) ∈ J_sp.
```

For a basis span, this is its defining finite decomposition relation;
rational scaling and addition extend it to the whole monoid algebra.
If `χ_sp(x)=0`, linearity gives `N(0)=0`, hence `x∈J_sp`. Equivalently,
the matrix units give independent coordinates for the resulting point-pair
normal form. This proves the reverse inclusion and the full kernel theorem.

The existing `CorrespondenceAlgebraPresentation` interface constructs the
quotient and its rational algebra structure. Its `lift` and `lift_unique`
supply the universal property, so no duplicate presentation interface is
introduced. The descended matrix map is injective by the kernel theorem
and surjective by the prior span-to-matrix surjectivity theorem; its algebra
equivalence is then constructed from this bijectivity.

## Next gap: actual Chow objects, then Betti

The exact additive quotient settles the first open item in `0008`. The
next two constructions are:

1. **Genuine zero-dimensional Chow identification.** Classify the actual
   integral closed subschemes of `Spec(k^I)` as reduced rational points and
   prove that there are no integral curves. Then specialize the definition
   of rational equivalence, identify the resulting genuine Chow groups,
   and compare their correspondence composition with this quotient map.
   [Stacks, Definition 42.19.1, Tag 02RW](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02RW)
   supplies the rational-equivalence definition; the absence of
   one-dimensional integral closed subschemes must still be matched to
   the formal geometric objects. For composition, use the fixed convention
   of rows as outputs and columns as inputs, matching
   [Stacks, Lemma 45.3.1, Tag 0FG0](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FG0).
2. **Genuine Betti and cycle-class comparison.** Construct the appropriate
   complex analytic realization and its rational cohomology, then the
   cycle-class and action comparisons and the Hodge subspace. Mathlib's
   singular degree-zero homology APIs can support the topological step.
   Identify the complex analytic point space and topology before applying
   that calculation to the Betti comparison.
