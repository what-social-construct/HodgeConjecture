# Canonical scheme complex points and the existing P¹ comparison

Date: 2026-11-08.

This advances item 4 of the [active checklist](../TARGET_BRIDGE.md).
The analytic point space and its rational singular cohomology are now
constructed for schemes over ℂ. The existing P¹ comparison and purity
proof are identified with this canonical target. General geometric
Betti–de Rham comparison and purity remain open.

## Constructed geometric target

`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.ComplexPoints f` consists of actual scheme
sections `p : Spec ℂ ⟶ X` with `p ≫ f = 𝟙`. Its topology is generated by
regular-function evaluations on affine opens, using the usual complex
topology. For every scheme open U, the subset U(ℂ) is open. Evaluations on
arbitrary opens are continuous; scheme morphisms over ℂ induce continuous
maps. Open and closed immersions induce the corresponding topological
embeddings. Affine presentations give homeomorphisms with the zero loci of
their actual polynomial relations.

These definitions and proofs are a narrow adaptation of AxiomMath/Weil,
revision `27cdb2049be62ee2911429b47f54774078450e32`; see the
[attribution and Apache license](../../third_party/axiom-weil/README.md).
The local modules depend on pinned Mathlib rather than Weil's monolithic
definitions, metadata or Stacks dependencies. No external theorem is
installed as an axiom or a new hypothesis package.

[SchemeBetti](../../HodgeConjecture/Realization/Betti/Scheme.lean) bundles
the resulting functor from schemes over Spec ℂ to `TopCat`. Its cohomology
is an abbreviation for the existing rational `SingularCochains.Cohomology`
on that space in every degree. Scheme pullback is the existing continuous
singular pullback; identity and composition prove the contravariant
`cohomologyFunctor`. There is no second cochain or quotient construction.

The general scheme filtration adapter now has canonical specializations
`complexPointsHodgeFiltration`, `complexPointsRationalHodgeClasses` and
their inverse-comparison membership theorem. They require the comparison
isomorphism explicitly, but no longer ask for a separate topological space.
They make no claim that an arbitrary linear equivalence is a geometric
period comparison.

## Agreement with existing models

For every complex algebra A, actual sections of Spec A over Spec ℂ are
homeomorphic to `A →ₐ[ℂ] ℂ` with the independently defined topology induced
by evaluation of all a ∈ A. `Spec`, its inverse on affine morphisms and
the natural global-section isomorphism prove that regular evaluation is
the original algebra homomorphism. The evaluation topology is local to
this model; no global instance on all algebra homomorphisms is installed.

For P¹ the new equivalence is the identity on each original scheme
morphism. The two old affine charts become open embeddings in the
canonical point topology. They cover it and give a quotient map, proving
continuity of the inverse. Thus the old chart topology and the new
regular-function topology agree by proof, rather than defining one by
transport from the other. Finite points, infinity and the old charts keep
their original morphisms.

## P¹ period and purity on the canonical target

The homeomorphism induces genuine rational singular-cohomology
equivalences by the original pullbacks, in every degree. Scalar extension
of the H² equivalence transports the existing boundary-period comparison
to `ℂ ⊗[ℚ] SchemeBetti.Cohomology f 2` for the actual P¹ structure map f.
The original Laurent integral and Chern period 2πi are retained. The
normalized and unnormalized ordinary Chow squares commute with this
identification using the old cycle map followed by the same pullback.

[HodgeTransport](../../HodgeConjecture/Realization/HodgeTransport.lean)
proves that scalar extension of a rational equivalence preserves canonical
conjugation, opposedness and the rational target. Applying it to the
actual cohomology equivalence proves purity of this canonical P¹ H².
Its filtration is proved equal to the image of the original derived
scheme filtration through the constructed comparison. This is a transport
of the already proved P¹ case, not a new general purity result.

## Source audit and remaining work

The pinned Stacks reuse audit was rechecked against Stacks revision
`5ed82d14634e8d9576bb844c7bd4e3da66d450e7`. `DeRham/RealizationComparison`
compares module-sheaf hypercohomology with underlying abelian-sheaf
hypercohomology on the same algebraic space. It does not give the desired
singular period map. The Hodge-Weil and characteristic-zero spreading
arguments retain substantive `hbetti` and `hDI` hypotheses respectively.

Weil's `ComplexPoints/AnalyticSpace` supplies topological sheaf machinery,
and `Betti/ArtinComparison` compares étale torsion cohomology with analytic
sheaf cohomology. Neither endpoint is the existing algebraic de Rham
hypercohomology versus the genuine rational singular tensor required here.
Only the complex-point definitions and topology/functoriality proofs are
ported in this checkpoint.

The next concrete object is a holomorphic de Rham complex on these
canonical point spaces, retaining a map from the existing algebraic forms.
Its local Poincaré comparison with constant coefficients, the connection
from analytic sheaf cohomology to our singular complex, and the proper
algebraic/analytic comparison must identify actual maps. General purity
also requires finite-dimensionality, boundedness and opposedness of the
resulting filtration; degeneration or a numerical equality alone is not
the asserted opposedness theorem. These remain item 4. Actual general Chow
construction/detection and class-dependent coverage remain item 5.
